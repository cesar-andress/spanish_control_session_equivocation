\begin{abstract}
Oral control sessions require the Spanish Prime Minister to respond to
registered questions from deputies. This study measures how often the first
response is an explicit reply, a partial reply, or an absence of reply, using
linked Congreso and ParlaMint-ES materials for the XIV legislature. The
pre-outcome main frame comprises $N=97$ exchanges after restoring thirty
linkage-QA cases that were never reply-coded and retaining three Phase-0 probe
exclusions. Formation-vote alignment is used only as a descriptive grouping;
confrontational formulation is strongly imbalanced by alignment, so alignment
differences are not interpretable as clean causal effects.
